# Terrain: landform, mountain biomes and the treeline

How the pipeline turns elevation and model climate into the `landform`
property and the biome on mountain hexes (`pipeline/elevation.py`,
`pipeline/paleobiome.py`, `pipeline/compose.py`; settings in
`pipeline/config.toml` under `[mountains]`, `[mountains.treeline]`,
`[paleobiome]` and `[paleobiome.lapse]`).

## Decisions

| Question | Choice | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Is "Mountains" a biome? | No. It's a static `landform` (0 flat, 1 hills, 2 mountains, 3 high mountains), and every land hex has a biome in `terrain`. | A mountain range carries forest, steppe or tundra depending on climate. The drawn maps' grey Mountains hid that, e.g. 63% of the Iranian plateau had no biome. |
| What makes a hex mountains? | At least 40% of its ETOPO1 cells pass the Kapos et al. (2000) bands; high mountains also have a median of at least 2000 m; hills have at least 15%. | The Kapos bands are the UNEP-WCMC mountain definition. The 2000 m and 15% cut-offs are this project's own. |
| What biome goes under the drawn Mountains? | They are biome gaps, like `Land`: the calibrated kNN predicts them from model climate (Beyer et al. 2020), moved to each hex's own elevation. | That's the same method the Land gaps already used, and it was validated there (74–80% held-out agreement). |
| Moving climate to the hex's elevation | Temperatures shift by −6.5 °C per km of difference between the hex's median elevation and the mean land elevation of the 0.5° model cells it samples. | Beyer et al. bias-correct the model to 0.5° modern observations, so a cell's climate stands for roughly that cell's mean elevation. 6.5 °C/km is the standard environmental lapse rate. Precipitation is left alone. |
| Köppen on mountains | Each hex's own mean monthly climate is classified with the same temperature shift, rather than the majority class of its model cells. | That keeps climate consistent with the Alpine biome: 85–94% of Alpine hexes come out ET/EF, depending on the key frame. |
| Which mountain hexes are Alpine? | Those whose lapse-corrected monthly climate fails the Paulsen & Körner (2014) treeline rule: a season of months above 0.9 °C must last at least 94 days and average at least 6.4 °C. | That rule is fitted to 376 treelines worldwide. Monthly means stand in for the daily values it was fitted on. |

## Checks (build of 2026-11-12, −6000)

- The Alps' highest hexes (median 2270–2370 m) come out Alpine. The present-day Alpine treeline is about 2000–2300 m.
- The highest Caucasus hexes (median 2400–2700 m) have season means of 7–10 °C and stay forest. The present-day treeline there is about 2500–2800 m, so a hex median sits at or below it.
- Central Afghanistan (2700–2900 m) stays semi-desert or steppe. Dry continental interiors have treelines above 3300 m.
- Alpine hexes number 141 at −9000, 98 at −6000 and 111 at −3000, tracking the early-to-mid Holocene warmth.

## Known limits

- The hex median decides the class, so the alpine fringe of a range whose median is below the treeline doesn't show.
- The climate model's own 0.5° biomes (`kept model` in the report) are not lapse-corrected.
- Köppen uses the same correction (`[koeppen] lapse`). It raises agreement with the modern Köppen map from 67% to 70% on mountain hexes and from 57% to 61% on high mountains (84% to 85% overall). Precipitation isn't corrected, so orographic rain and rain shadows still come from the 0.5° model.

## Islands, drawn water and settled land

The map build fixes four kinds of water that the hex majority vote and the
drawn key frames get wrong. Each has a section in `build/report.md`.

| Question | Choice | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Drawn classes copied wrong (`[[terrain.reinterpret]]`) | Drawn Savannah north of 30°N, and drawn Chaparral north of 39°N and east of 26°E, become Grassland. | The composite key frames were copied from Adams & Faure's QEN maps (`raw/maps/europe/`). QEN's pale-yellow steppe over the Pontic–Caspian region, the Caucasus and Anatolia (the 12–11 ka and 5 ka maps) was copied as red Chaparral at −9000 and yellow Savannah from −8000 on (275 hexes at −8000, 1,002 at −5000). The gap filling then spread both classes over the Anatolian, Zagros and Talesh uplands. Tropical savanna doesn't grow there, and the literature anchors (Shotota, Lake Neor, Nar Gölü) show steppe, meadow and oak parkland. The fix raised agreement with the literature anchors from 28% to 43% of slices, and the Savannah contradictions fell from 25 to 1. |
| Mis-dated key frames in Europe (`[[terrain.misdated]]`) | In Europe, the Caucasus and Anatolia (four boxes), every drawn class at −9000 except Water and Ice, and every one at −8000 except Water, becomes a gap filled from the climate model. The calibration for those years also learns from the −7000 drawing of the same boxes. At −8000 the model's 10 ka land ice replaces the drawn ice. −10000 reuses the −9000 drawing, so it gets the same fill. | The QEN Europe originals (`raw/maps/europe/`) print their radiocarbon ages. The −9000 composite copies the 12,000–11,000 ¹⁴C BP map (Allerød, about −11950 to −10950) and the −8000 composite the 11,000–10,000 ¹⁴C BP map (Younger Dryas, which ended about −9750; Walker et al. 2009). The −7000 composite copies the 8,000 ¹⁴C BP map (about −6950) and is dated right. Against the pollen anchors, Europe north of 42°N agreed with the map in 23–25% of slices at −10000 to −9000 and in 46–52% at −8500 and −8000; with the fix, 44%, 51%, 65%, 77% and 81% from −10000 to −8000. From −7000 on it stays at 85–91%. The Balkan "Chaparral" at −8000 was the Younger Dryas steppe, and the boreal belt at −8000 was the Younger Dryas pink belt. The model's 11 ka ice put 9 anchors that record vegetation under ice (Finnmark coast, southern Finland and Norway), against 3 for the drawn Allerød ice, so the drawn ice is kept at −9000. The African parts of these composites are not changed: their source maps aren't in `raw/maps/` with dates, and there are too few anchors to judge them. |
| Irano-Anatolian uplands (`[biome.upland]`) | A gap-filled hex in 26–62°E, 30–43.5°N, at or above 700 m, with a coldest month at or below −2 °C and at least 200 mm of rain, becomes Woodlands from −7000 where rain reaches 350 mm, else Steppe-Forest from 225 mm, else Grassland. Rain and temperature are the model's BIO12 and lapse-corrected BIO6. | The drawn maps leave these uplands as "Mountains". The gap filler had never seen woodland in this climate, so it made them Chaparral, Savannah or Grassland. The records (Nar, Hazar, Mirabad, Hashilan, Gölhisar, Zeribar, Van, Urmia, Abant, Yeniçağa) show steppe and forest-steppe until about −7000, then oak woodland where wet and steppe where dry. The mild-winter test leaves the Lebanon and Aegean mountains Mediterranean. The thresholds were fitted on the anchors that aren't held out (52 of 60 upland keyframe slices). On held-out anchors: upland literature keyframe slices from 15 to 37 of 44; all held-out literature slices from 45% to 65%; held-out pollen labels in the box from 33 to 59 of 85; the whole map's held-out pollen labels from 347 to 373 of 544, with no region worse. |
| Gap filling and the literature (`[paleobiome.calibrate]`) | The literature anchors that aren't held out are added to the gap filler's training, each slice 5 times. There is no distance limit on which drawn hexes vote. | The drawn maps leave the Irano-Anatolian uplands as "Mountains", so the gap filler never sees oak woodland in their climate and borrows Chaparral from Iberia and the Maghreb. A 1,000–2,500 km limit made things worse on the held-out anchors (10 of 31 keyframe slices right fell to 7–8). Adding the literature corrects the map around those sites (27 to 46 of 57) but leaves the held-out sites unchanged (10 of 31). It is a local correction, not a cure; the uplands rule below supplies the missing class. |
| Sahel and Nubia in the humid period (`[[terrain.reinterpret]]`, Grassland → Savannah) | Drawn Grassland in 20–38°E, 15–22°N at the −7000 and −6000 key frames becomes Savannah. | Here the map follows the drawn key frames, not the climate model. They draw grassland where pollen at El Atrun (18°N), Oyo (19°N) and Selima (21.4°N) shows thorn-tree savanna at the humid-period maximum. The timing was fitted on the training sites. The northern limit was not well constrained (19.5–24.5°N fit equally), so it was set at 22°N, just north of Selima, without consulting the held-out sites. On the held-out NE Africa sites (Oyo, Nabta, Sodmein): 5 to 7 of 10 slices. All held-out literature slices: 65% to 68%. The pollen anchors are unchanged. Arabia's mismatches run both ways (Awafi and Wadi Sana too dry; al-Hawa, Khor and Rub' al Khali too wet) at too few sites for a rule, so Arabia is left as it is. |
| Drawn Water on high ground (`[terrain.high_water]`) | Drawn Water on a hex with a median elevation over 200 m, outside every lake box in `data/lakes/lakes.toml`, becomes a gap filled from the climate model. | These are artefacts: coasts drawn a hex off in the Rif, and one-key-frame flickers in the Atlas at 1000 m. The report lists each one. Saharan cases (northern Chad at about 450 m, Mauritania, Algeria) may be real palaeolakes. One that is confirmed goes in `lakes.toml` with a level curve and sources, and that brings it back. |
| Drawn Water on mostly-land hexes (`[islands] restore_majority_land`) | Drawn Water on a hex that is at least 50% land at the frame's water level, and outside every modelled lake shoreline, becomes land. | The drawn coast is a hex off. Where a modelled lake (Caspian, Baltic, Mega-Chad) disagrees with the drawing, the model wins, as in the lakes stage. |
| Islands (`[islands]`) | Hexes with at least 10% land at the frame's water level are grouped into landmasses. One of at most 60 hexes with at least 150 km² of land keeps max(1, round(area / hex area)) land hexes. | Gotland, Bornholm, Menorca and Malta had vanished. A lower threshold merges Bornholm into Scania and Menorca into Mallorca. Orkney is still part of Britain at hex scale. |
| Which water level? | A lake's level counts only inside that year's modelled shoreline; elsewhere it is global sea level. Surfaces are corrected for uplift still to come. | The Baltic's box also takes in the Arctic coast, and Mega-Chad's box takes in dry desert. |
| Settlements and water (`[settled_land]`) | A settlement's hex stays land in any frame where the map has it Water but the site's ground is above the water. The ground is the highest ETOPO1 cell within one cell of the site, less uplift. The water is the lake inside its shoreline; or the sea where the hex is ocean-connected, or a neighbour is and the ground is within 20 m of sea level; or nothing. The phase ends when the water rises 3 m beyond the depth the model gave at the phase's start. | A dated site was dry when it was occupied, so the model's depth there at the start is its own error. Shore sites sit within metres of the water, closer than the models are good. The Jordan Valley and Qattara are below sea level but aren't the sea. Islets too small for the grid (Youra, Scilly) keep their hex. |
| Settlement coordinates | Fixes in `data/settlements/corrections.toml`, sourced in `data/settlements/CORRECTIONS.md`. The audit of what is still suspect is in `data/settlements/coordinate_audit.csv`. | Sites at −1000 m or in the Channel are coordinate errors. They would otherwise drown, or pull the coast out to sea. |

### Checks (build of 2026-11-12)

- Land hexes at −6000: Gotland 3, Bornholm 1, Menorca 1, Malta 1, Mallorca 2, Crete 4 and Cyprus 6. Before the fix: 1, 0, 0, 0, 1, 3 and 6.
- 15 occupation phases end in the water. Most are Ertebølle shore sites on the Danish Littorina coast, and Doggerland. Before the margin and the shoreline rule there were 60, including Finnish and Norwegian shore sites on rising coasts.
- 41 phases start under the modelled water. These are coordinate errors, plus the lake and uplift cases listed in the report. The Baltic and Finnish ones are evidence against `[isostasy]`.

## Biome anchors

`python -m pipeline anchors` (`pipeline/anchors.py`, settings under `[anchors]`) picks pollen records to check the biome maps against, gives each a biome per 500-year slice, and lists the parts of the map with none. Output: `anchors.csv` (one row per anchor), `anchor_biomes.csv` (one row per anchor, slice and method) and `anchors_report.md` (counts, the 6 ka cross-check, anchors on Water, and the gap list). Nothing in the build reads these yet; they are for a scorer and a critic.

| Question | Choice | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Which records? | EMBSeCBIO fossil entities, and Neotoma/EPD pollen datasets elsewhere. A Neotoma record within 0.05° of an EMBSeCBIO one counts as the same core. | EMBSeCBIO covers the core region (28–49°N, 20–62°E) with IntCal20 age models and basin sizes; Neotoma covers the rest of the box. |
| What makes a record good enough? | Its levels span ≥ 80% of −10000…−3000, with no gap over 1000 years and at least 15 levels. They are ranked on coverage, resolution, the longest gap and (EMBSeCBIO only) basin size. | A big lake shows the region's vegetation; a small bog mostly shows its own shore. |
| How many per area? | The best 2 in each 5° cell. | Without thinning, a single cell in France and the Alps has 85 qualifying cores and the SE has 18 in all. |
| Published labels first | The BIOME 6000 classified plotfile (Harrison 2017), for a site within 0.06° of an anchor, mapped to game classes through its BIOME4 codes and `[paleobiome.classes]`. | These are expert biomisations, but they exist only for 6 ka BP, so they label only the −4000 slice. |
| Fallback: biomise the pollen | The method of Cruz-Silva et al. (2022), with their published training set and ROC thresholds. Each biome is the mean and SD of each taxon's percentage over modern samples in that biome. A sample's distance to a biome is √Σ(p − mean)² / (SD + 0.5)², its score is exp(−distance / 100), and it takes the best-scoring biome. A score below that biome's threshold flags a no-analogue assemblage. | This is a published method with its inputs published, so no taxon table has to be invented. It beat classic biomisation in this region (77% vs 65% balanced accuracy). |
| Taxa | EMBSeCBIO anchors use the paper's own harmonised fossil percentages. Neotoma anchors are re-fetched with full taxa and harmonised to the 242 training taxa (`harmonise`). Levels with under 70% of their pollen and spores harmonised are dropped. | Unqualified *Quercus* counts as deciduous and unqualified *Pinus* as diploxylon. That's right north of the Mediterranean. Mapping plain *Pinus* to the training set's "Pinus" (an EMBSeCBIO-only type) pushed boreal samples toward the wrong biomes: fixing it raised the 6 ka cross-check from 42% to 62%. |
| Slice label | The commonest biome among samples within ±250 years of the slice; ties go to the higher mean score. Its share, score and no-analogue share are kept. | This turns noisy level-by-level labels into one call per slice, and keeps how confident that call is. |
| Gaps | A 5° cell with ≥ 20 land hexes and no time-series anchor. The first BIOME 6000 site in a gap cell is added as a 6 ka-only anchor. | The remaining gaps are the research list: records that exist in the literature but not in these databases. |
| Hold-out | An anchor is held out when the crc32 of its id is 0 mod 3. | A scorer that is tuned on the other anchors can be tested on these honestly. |
| Water | Each anchor-slice row gives the map's terrain on the anchor's hex, whether that is Water, and the terrain of the nearest land hex that slice. | Lake and marine cores sit in water by nature, and their pollen describes the land around them. A land site on Water means the drawn coast or lake is wrong. |

### Checks (run of 2026-11-12)

- 504 records qualify; 128 are kept (99 EPD, 20 EMBSeCBIO, 6 African Pollen Database, 3 Alpine Palynological Database), plus 32 BIOME 6000 6 ka-only anchors in gap cells. 56 of the 160 are held out.
- 24 anchors have both a published and a biomised 6 ka label. They give the same main game class for 15 (62%), and the class lists overlap for 20 (83%). The paper reports 65–77% for the method on modern samples.
- 7% of biomised slice labels are flagged no-analogue.
- 30 anchors sit on a Water hex in at least one slice. Most are lakes, marine cores and Baltic sites, but Majen Ben H'mida (Tunisia, 9 slices) and Lago Trifoglietti (Calabria) are on land.

| Left out | An anchor with no land hex within 0.6° is off the grid and is dropped. Biomised labels are not used more than 300 km from every training sample (`out_of_domain`), or for anchors in `[[anchors.exclude]]`. Samples that are over 40% Cyperaceae are left out. | The method has no biomes for tropical Africa or the far north-east. A mire's own sedges swamp the regional signal: Hawks Tor is 62% Cyperaceae at −3800, and BIOME 6000's "tundra" there contradicts every other SW England site. |
| Literature anchors | `anchors_literature.csv`: 24 records from the published literature for the SE gap cells (Iran, Anatolia, Arabia, NE Africa, the Caucasus and SE Caspian) and the Middle Volga (two archaeological pollen sites and one regional synthesis), one row per record and slice with the classes the paper's vegetation fits, best first. | Gathered per region; each record kept only if its abstract or text states the vegetation for dated periods, its DOI resolves on Crossref with the title read, and each slice falls in a dated phase (BCE = 1950 − cal BP). Four Saharan records give only uncalibrated 14C ages; their slices use an approximate calibration and say so. Slices taken from a review rather than the record's own paper cite the review. |
| Core region | Up to 4 per cell inside the EMBSeCBIO box. Hand-entered literature anchors (`anchors_literature.csv`, with references listed here) fill gap cells. | The SE is the game's focus and has the fewest records. |

### Known limits

- The training set is modern Europe and SW Asia west of 62°E. Africa south of the Sahara and the box east of 62°E have no training biomes for the tropics, so the African anchors' biomised labels are only a guess (Jikariya: savanna published, steppe biomised).
- The cell grid treats deep desert and taiga like the Fertile Crescent. Most of the 128 gap cells are the Sahara, Arabia and Russia, where pollen records are scarce.
- No sample weighting by age uncertainty: a level counts for the slice its mean age falls near.

## Massif sketch (board peaks)

The board's peaks stand on a per-hex sketch of the high ground,
`elevation.massif` (config `[mountains.massif]`), made from the same ETOPO1
cells as the landform. Hills and mountain hexes get one; flat hexes don't.
It is derived geometry for drawing, not data about the past, and it never
replaces `landform`.

| Part | How | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Summits | Up to three local maxima (highest within 3 cells), at least 150 m above the hex's 10th-percentile floor and 0.45 hex-local units apart. | Peaks stand where the mountains are, not at a hashed spot, so they keep off the valley floors where the rivers run. |
| Strike | The structure tensor of the gradient over the cells above the median ([structure tensor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Structure_tensor)). The ridge is 90° from the dominant slope direction; coherence is (λ1 − λ2)/(λ1 + λ2). | Ranges are drawn stretched along their real grain, and neighbouring hexes agree, so they chain. |
| Kind | Plateau: median ≥ 900 m, relief < 450 m. Cone: one summit ≥ 1.8× the next, relief ≥ 900 m, coherence < 0.35. Ridge: coherence ≥ 0.3. Otherwise massif. | Central Anatolia reads as high open ground, Erciyes and Süphan as lone volcanoes, the Taurus and Pontic ranges as chains. The thresholds are this project's own. |

### Checks (prototype run of 2026-11-13, the current grid)

2477 ridge, 1126 massif, 299 plateau and 229 cone hexes. Probes: Erciyes
is a cone with its summit about 3300 m (ETOPO1's 2′ cells smooth the
3917 m peak). The western Anatolian horsts (Bozdağ) strike about E–W, the
Bolkar Taurus NE–SW, Lebanon and the Alps NE–SW, and the Jordan rift
escarpment N–S. The Konya plain and the central plateau are plateau.

### Known limits

- Hasan Dağ comes out a massif, not a cone: its neighbour Melendiz falls in the
  same hex.
- Ağrı (Ararat) is a ridge, because Little Ararat lines up with it.
- A summit on a hex's edge belongs to whichever hex holds its highest cell.
